Next door to history: Marconi, May 1897

In May 1897 Guglielmo Marconi and William Preece, engineer-in-chief of the Post Office, tested wireless telegraphy across the Bristol Channel at Lavernock Point, about 5 km south of Llandough.

Signals crossed open sea from Flat Holm island to Lavernock, and on 18 May from Lavernock to Brean Down in Somerset. A plaque at St Lawrence’s church, Lavernock, marks the trials.

The tour will tell this story beside the farm’s own.

Sources

  • Evening Express, 15 May 1897, p. 4, “Wireless telegraphy: Marconi invention tested at Lavernock” (Welsh Newspapers Online)
  • South Wales Echo, 27 May 1897, p. 4: the signed tape “Sent from Lavernock Point … received at Brean Down, May 18th, 1897” (Welsh Newspapers Online)
  • Plaque, St Lawrence’s church, Lavernock: “Between Lavernock and Flat Holm 11th May, Lavernock and Brean Down 18th May 1897” (Read the Plaque)
